# Cold Call Script: Contractors (Website + Local SEO)

**Who to call:** roofers, plumbers, electricians, HVAC, landscapers, renovators and general contractors in Halton and Hamilton (Waterdown, Burlington, Oakville, Milton, Hamilton).

**Before you dial (60 seconds):** Google "[their trade] [their town]". Note three things:
1. Do they show up on page one or in the map pack? Who does?
2. Do they have a website? Does it work on a phone? Is there a "call" button and a quote form?
3. How many Google reviews do they have compared with the top competitor?

You'll use one of these in the hook. A specific observation beats a generic pitch every time.

**Goal of the call:** book a 15-minute look at their Google presence. Don't sell the website on this call.

---

## 1. Opener (10 seconds)

> "Hi, is this [Name]? It's [Your Name], I'm a local web designer here in [Town]. I'll be quick. I was searching for a [trade] in [Town] this morning and noticed something about your business. Got 30 seconds?"

*If they say they're on a job:* "No problem, when's a better time, after 5 or tomorrow morning?" Book it and hang up.

## 2. The hook (pick the one that fits what you found)

**No website / Facebook only:**
> "When I searched '[trade] in [Town]', you didn't come up. [Competitor] did, right at the top. People who search like that are ready to hire this week, and right now those calls are going to them."

**Old or broken site:**
> "I pulled up your site on my phone and [the call button doesn't work / it's hard to read / there's no way to request a quote]. Most people searching for a [trade] are on their phone, so a lot of them leave and call the next guy."

**Not ranking:**
> "You've got a website, but when I search '[trade] [Town]' you're on page [2/3]. [Competitor] is in the top three on the map. That spot is where most of the calls come from."

## 3. The question (then stop talking)

> "Where does most of your work come from right now? Word of mouth, or do you get calls from Google too?"

Listen. Whatever they say, follow up with:

> "And if you could pick up a few more jobs a month, would you have room for them, or are you booked solid?"

## 4. The pitch (keep it short)

> "That's what I do. I build websites for local businesses that are made to get the phone ringing: fast on a phone, a big call button, a quote form, and set up so Google shows you when people in [Town] search for a [trade]. I also get your Google Business Profile in shape, since that's what puts you on the map.
>
> I recently finished a site for A&R LLP, an accounting firm in Waterdown. I can send you the link so you can see the kind of work I do."

## 5. The ask

> "What I'd like to do is take 15 minutes, show you exactly where you show up on Google right now, and where your competitors are beating you. No charge. If it makes sense to work together, great. If not, you keep the notes. Does [day] at [time] work, or is [other day] better?"

Always offer two specific times.

---

## Objections

**"I get all my work from word of mouth."**
> "That's the best kind of work. Here's the thing though: when someone gets your name from a friend, most of them Google you before they call. If they find nothing, or an old site, some of them call someone else. A good site backs up the referral."

**"I already have a website."**
> "Good, that's a head start. Is it bringing you calls? When I searched [trade] [Town] you were on page [X]. The 15 minutes would show you what's holding it back, even if you fix it yourself."

**"How much does it cost?"**
> "It depends on what you need, and I don't want to guess before I've looked. That's what the 15 minutes is for. I'll give you a straight number at the end. Most contractors make it back with one or two jobs."

*(Only say the last line if your pricing supports it.)*

**"I'm too busy."**
> "That's a good problem to have. Is it busy all year, or are there slow months? A site and SEO take a few months to build up, so the time to start is before the slow season, not during it."

**"Send me an email."**
> "Happy to. So I send something useful, what's the main thing you'd want more of: more jobs, bigger jobs, or a certain type of work?" Get the email, send the competitor screenshot that day, and call back in two days.

**"Not interested."**
> "Fair enough. Can I ask, is it because you've got enough work, or you've been burned by a marketing company before?" If they've been burned: "I hear that a lot. That's why I start with a free look and no contract." If they still say no, thank them and move on.

## Voicemail (under 20 seconds)

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name], a local web designer in [Town]. I searched '[trade] in [Town]' and noticed [Competitor] is showing up ahead of you. I've got a couple of quick ideas to fix that. Call me back at [number]. Again, [Your Name], [number]."

## Follow-up text (send same day)

> "Hi [Name], [Your Name] here, I left you a voicemail. Here's what I found when I searched [trade] in [Town]: [screenshot]. Happy to walk you through it in 15 min, no charge. [Your number]"

---

## Quick tips

- Call 7–8 am or 4–6 pm. Contractors are on jobs midday.
- Stand up and smile. It comes through on the phone.
- Say the competitor's name. It gets attention faster than anything else.
- Don't promise rankings or a set number of leads. Promise what you control: a fast site built to convert, and proper local SEO setup.
- Track every call: name, trade, town, outcome, callback date.
